# Formula 1 (F1, Formula One): FIA 2026 Sporting Regulations and ISC Appendix L (excerpts)

Excerpts and close paraphrases of two FIA documents, checked against the official PDFs from fia.com on
12 October 2026:

- FIA 2026 F1 Regulations, Section B (Sporting), Issue 09, dated 1 October 2026
- Appendix L to the FIA International Sporting Code, Chapter IV (code of driving conduct on circuits), 2026
  edition published 23 June 2026

Sentences in quotation marks are the FIA's own words. The article numbers are the official ones: `FIA-B1.9.3` is
Section B Article B1.9.3, and `FIA-L4.2b` is Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 b). (The prefix isn't `F1-` because
the council reads F1 as "established fact 1".) The FIA reissues the Sporting Regulations several times a season,
so check https://www.fia.com/regulation/category/110 for a later issue. Not covered: technical rules, the 2026
active aero and Overtake energy rules (Article B7, set electronically), and the stewards' driving standards
guidelines, which aren't part of either document.

In the regulations, a race or sprint is a "TTCS" (total time classified session) and practice or qualifying is an
"LTCS" (lap time classified session); the excerpts below say race, sprint, practice and qualifying instead.

## FIA-B1.8.3 Driving behaviour follows the International Sporting Code
Source: Section B, Article B1.8.3
"Drivers must observe the provisions of the ISC relating to driving behaviour on circuits at all times." Those
provisions are Appendix L, Chapter IV (the FIA-L4 clauses below).

## FIA-B1.8.4 Yellow flags
Source: Section B, Article B1.8.4
Light panels and the steering-wheel display have the same meaning as flags. Single waved yellow: the driver must
reduce speed and be prepared to change direction; to satisfy the stewards, the driver is expected to have braked
earlier and/or discernibly reduced speed in that marshalling sector. Double waved yellow: the driver must reduce
speed significantly and be prepared to change direction or stop; it must be clear the driver has not attempted to
set a meaningful lap time on that lap, and in qualifying or sprint qualifying any lap through a double waved yellow
sector is deleted. Under a Safety Car or Virtual Safety Car, a driver passing double waved yellows must also stay
above the minimum time set by the FIA ECU in that sector.

## FIA-B1.8.5 Driving slowly, erratically or dangerously
Source: Section B, Article B1.8.5
"At no time may an F1 Car be driven unnecessarily slowly, erratically or in a manner which could be deemed
potentially dangerous to other drivers or any other person."

## FIA-B1.8.6 Track limits and rejoining the track
Source: Section B, Article B1.8.6
"Drivers must make every reasonable effort to use the track at all times and may not leave the track without a
justifiable reason." A driver has left the track if no part of the car remains in contact with it; the white
lines defining the track edges are part of the track, but the kerbs are not. A car that leaves the track may
rejoin, but only when it is safe to do so and "without gaining any lasting advantage". At the absolute discretion
of the Race Director, a driver may be given the opportunity to give back the whole of any advantage gained by
leaving the track.

## FIA-B1.9.3 When the stewards may penalise a driver
Source: Section B, Article B1.9.3
Whether a driver involved in an incident is penalised is at the stewards' discretion. "Unless it is clear to the
stewards that a driver was wholly or predominantly to blame for an Incident no penalty will be imposed." The
stewards may use the penalties in the F1 regulations in addition to, or instead of, those available under the
International Sporting Code.

## FIA-B1.9.4 Penalties in practice and qualifying
Source: Section B, Article B1.9.4
For an incident in practice or qualifying, the stewards may delete a driver's lap time (or lap times) or drop the
driver as many grid positions as they consider appropriate. Unless it is completely clear that a driver committed a
driving infringement, such incidents are normally investigated after the session. Grid penalties are served in the
race, or in the sprint if the infringement happened in sprint qualifying.

## FIA-B1.9.5 Penalties in a race or sprint
Source: Section B, Article B1.9.5
For an incident in a race or sprint, the stewards may impose any one of: (a) a 5-second penalty, served by stopping
in the pit box for at least five seconds; (b) a 10-second penalty, the same for ten seconds; (c) a drive-through
penalty; (d) a ten-second stop-and-go penalty; (e) a time penalty; (f) a driver reprimand (a driver's fifth
reprimand of the championship, if at least four were for driving infringements, brings a ten-place grid penalty);
(g) a team reprimand; (h) a drop of any number of grid positions at the driver's next sprint or race within twelve
months; (i) disqualification from the results; (j) suspension from the driver's next competition.

## FIA-B1.9.6 Serving a penalty
Source: Section B, Article B1.9.6
A 5- or 10-second penalty is served at the driver's next pit stop (not when only following the Safety Car through
the pit lane or entering it under a red flag); a driver who makes no further stop has the 5 or 10 seconds added to
their race time instead. A drive-through or stop-and-go must be served after crossing the Control Line no more than
twice once the team is notified, and can't be started under the VSC or Safety Car. If a drive-through or stop-and-go
is imposed in the last three laps, 20 or 30 seconds respectively is added to the driver's race time instead; any of
these penalties imposed after the session ends is converted into added time (5, 10, 20 or 30 seconds). The car
may not be worked on while it is stationary serving a penalty.

## FIA-B1.9.7 Penalties that can't be appealed
Source: Section B, Article B1.9.7
Appeals may not be made against: penalties under Article B1.9.5 (a) to (h), including those imposed in the last
three laps or after the session; grid drops for power-unit changes; penalties in practice or qualifying under
Article B1.9.4; and certain other decisions listed in the article. Disqualification and suspension (B1.9.5 i and j)
are not on this list.

## FIA-B1.6.2 Unsafe release from the pit stop or garage
Source: Section B, Article B1.6.2
A car is released when it is driven out of its garage area, or after a pit stop when its rear axle has passed the
position its front axle occupied while stationary. Cars "must not be released from a garage or Designated Pit Stop
Position in a manner that creates an unsafe situation", in any way that could endanger pit lane personnel or
another driver, or with a clear and immediate risk of contact with, or that is likely to cause damage to, another
car. Cars also must not be released in an unsafe condition (for example a loose wheel): in practice or qualifying
the driver may get a grid drop; in a race or sprint the driver gets a stop-and-go penalty (or the team a fine if
the car retires as a result); and a driver who keeps driving knowing the car was released in an unsafe condition
gets an additional penalty.

## FIA-B1.6.3 Pit lane speed limit
Source: Section B, Article B1.6.3
"A speed limit of 80km/h will be imposed in the Pit Lane during the whole Competition", unless the Race Director
amends it. Speeding in practice or qualifying costs the team a fine of 100 euros per km/h over the limit, up to
1,000 euros, plus a possible further penalty if the stewards suspect it was done to gain an advantage. In a race or
sprint, the stewards may impose a 5-second, 10-second, drive-through or stop-and-go penalty. A car may never be
reversed in the pit lane under its own power.

## FIA-B5.12 Virtual Safety Car (VSC)
Source: Section B, Article B5.12
The VSC is used when double waved yellow flags are needed and competitors or officials may be in danger, but a
Safety Car isn't warranted. While it is in use, no car may be driven unnecessarily slowly, erratically or
dangerously; every car must reduce speed and stay above the minimum time set by the FIA ECU at least once in each
marshalling sector and at both safety car lines, and also when the panels turn green (penalty: 5-second, 10-second,
drive-through or stop-and-go). No driver may overtake another car on the track, except: when entering the pits,
after reaching the first safety car line; when leaving the pits, before reaching the second safety car line;
between cars that are both in the pit entry road, pit lane or pit exit road; or when a car slows with an obvious
problem. In a race or sprint, cars may enter the pits under the VSC only to change tyres. After "VSC ENDING", the
panels turn green 10 to 15 seconds later and drivers may race immediately.

## FIA-B5.13.2 Safety Car: speed, queueing and overtaking
Source: Section B, Article B5.13.2
While the Safety Car is deployed, no car may be driven unnecessarily slowly, erratically or dangerously. All cars
must reduce speed and queue behind the Safety Car no more than ten car lengths apart (twenty in low visibility, at
the Race Director's discretion), and must stay above the FIA ECU minimum time in each marshalling sector until they
cross the first safety car line for the second time. No driver may overtake another car on the track, including
the Safety Car, until passing the Control Line for the first time after the Safety Car has entered the pit entry
road to return to the pit lane. The exceptions: when signalled by the Safety Car's green light; when entering the
pits, after the first safety car line; when leaving the pits, before the second safety car line; the Safety Car
itself once it reaches the first safety car line on its way in; between cars in the pit entry road, pit lane or pit
exit road; a car stopping at its garage while the Safety Car leads the field through the pit lane; and a car that
slows with an obvious problem. In a race or sprint, cars may enter the pits under the Safety Car only to change
tyres, unless the field is told to use the pit lane.

## FIA-B5.13.4 Safety Car: order of cars and lapped cars
Source: Section B, Article B5.13.4
(a) When the Race Director instructs, the Safety Car's green light signals cars between it and the leader to pass;
they then continue at reduced speed, without overtaking, to the back of the queue. (b) If the Race Director
considers the track unsuitable for overtaking, the message "OVERTAKING WILL NOT BE PERMITTED" is sent. (c) "If the
Race Director considers it safe to do so, the message 'LAPPED CARS MAY NOW OVERTAKE' will be sent to all
Competitors", and the green light signals all cars lapped by the leader to pass the cars on the lead lap and the
Safety Car. This applies only to cars that were lapped when they crossed the Control Line at the end of the lap in
which they crossed the first safety car line for the second time after the Safety Car was deployed. While they
pass, cars on the lead lap must stay on the racing line unless deviating is unavoidable. The unlapping cars then go
round without overtaking and take up position at the back of the queue.

## FIA-B5.13.5 How long the Safety Car stays out
Source: Section B, Article B5.13.5
(a) Except under Article B5.13.4c, the Safety Car stays out at least until the leader is behind it and all
remaining cars are queued behind them. (b) Unless the Race Director considers the Safety Car still necessary, once
"LAPPED CARS MAY NOW OVERTAKE" has been sent, the Safety Car returns to the pits at the end of the following lap.

## FIA-B5.13.6 Safety Car in this lap, and the restart
Source: Section B, Articles B5.13.6 and B5.13.8
The message "SAFETY CAR IN THIS LAP" and the Safety Car's orange lights going out signal that it will enter the pit
lane at the end of that lap. The first car in line may then dictate the pace and fall back more than ten car
lengths. From the lights going out until the Safety Car enters the pit entry road, drivers must proceed at a pace
with no erratic acceleration or braking, and no manoeuvre likely to endanger other drivers or impede the restart;
"no part of an F1 Car may be driven alongside any part of an F1 Car ahead." If the Safety Car is still out at the
start of the last lap, or is deployed during it, it enters the pit lane at the end of the lap and the cars take the
chequered flag without overtaking.

## FIA-B5.14.2 Red flag: suspending a race or sprint
Source: Section B, Article B5.14.2
When the red flag is shown, "Overtaking is forbidden." The pit exit is closed and all cars must proceed slowly into
the pit lane, lining up in the fast lane in the order they entered it (or on the grid, in the order they arrive, if
the pit entry has been closed). "If the TTCS cannot be resumed the results will be taken at the end of the
penultimate lap before the lap during which the signal to suspend the TTCS was given."

## FIA-L4.2a Being lapped, and blue flags
Source: ISC Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 a)
"A car alone on the track may use the full width of the said track, however, as soon as it is caught by a car which
is about to lap it the driver must allow the faster driver past at the first possible opportunity." If the driver
being caught doesn't seem to be using the mirrors, waved blue flags are shown; a driver who appears to ignore blue
flags is reported to the stewards.

## FIA-L4.2b Overtaking and defending a position
Source: ISC Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 b)
"Overtaking, according to the circumstances, may be carried out on either the right or the left. A driver may not
leave the track without justifiable reason. More than one change of direction to defend a position is not
permitted. Any driver moving back towards the racing line, having earlier defended his position off-line, should
leave at least one car width between his own car and the edge of the track on the approach to the corner. However,
manoeuvres liable to hinder other drivers, such as deliberate crowding of a car beyond the edge of the track or any
other abnormal change of direction, are strictly prohibited." A driver who appears guilty of any of these is
reported to the stewards.

## FIA-L4.2c Track limits under the International Sporting Code
Source: ISC Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 c)
Drivers must use the track at all times and may not leave it without a justifiable reason. The white lines
defining the track edges are part of the track, but the kerbs are not; any part of a kerb contained between the
white lines counts as track. A car that leaves the track for any reason may rejoin, but only when it is safe to do
so and without gaining any lasting advantage. A driver has left the track if no part of the car remains in contact
with it. A driver may be reported to the stewards for any act that brings debris onto the track.

## FIA-L4.2d Causing a collision
Source: ISC Appendix L, Chapter IV, Article 2 d)
"Causing a collision, repetition of serious mistakes or the appearance of a lack of control over the car (such as
leaving the track) will be reported to the Stewards and may entail the imposition of penalties up to and including
the disqualification of any driver concerned."
